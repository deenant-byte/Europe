Risk management
Position sizing
You don't decide how much to trade based on confidence. You decide based on how much you're willing to lose — then work backwards to find the position size.
Interactive calculator
The formula
Position size = (Capital × Risk%) ÷ Stop loss distance%
Example: Rp 4jt capital, 1% risk, 5% stop loss → Rp 40,000 at risk → Position size = Rp 800,000
Risk rules
Conservative (new traders)0.5–1% risk per trade
Moderate (experienced)1–2% risk per trade
Aggressive (risky)3–5% risk per trade
Never move stop loss further awayThis turns small losses into account-killers
Always set stop loss before enteringNot after. The plan comes first.
With 1% risk per trade, you can lose 20 trades in a row and still have 80% of your capital. With 10% risk, 10 losses wipes everything.
Technical analysis
Volume analysis
Price tells you what happened. Volume tells you how much the market meant it. A breakout without volume is almost always a trap.
Real breakout — price breaks resistance AND volume spikes. High confidence entry signal.
Fake breakout (trap) — price breaks resistance but volume stays low. Price reverses. No conviction.
Confirmed downtrend — high volume on red candles means sellers are in control. Don't buy here.
The 4 volume rules
Rule 1  High volume confirms a move
When price breaks a key level AND volume is 1.5–2× above average, the move is likely real. Big players are participating. That's conviction.
Rule 2  Low volume breakouts are traps
A breakout on thin volume usually means a few small traders pushed the price — not institutional buying. It typically reverses quickly. This is the "bull trap" or "fakeout."
Rule 3  Declining volume in a trend = warning
If price keeps rising but volume shrinks each candle, the trend is losing steam. Fewer buyers are participating. This often precedes a reversal.
Rule 4  Volume spikes at reversals
A massive volume candle at key support with a long wick often signals reversal. One side tried hard to push through and failed — "exhaustion volume."

Candlestick charts
Every candle tells a story of a battle between buyers and sellers. Learn to read them and you can see who is winning in real time.

Key candle patterns
Hammer bullish
Small body at top, long lower wick. Means sellers pushed price down hard but buyers fought back. Strong reversal signal at support.
Shooting star bearish
Small body at bottom, long upper wick. Buyers pushed price up but sellers rejected it. Strong reversal signal at resistance.
Bullish engulfing bullish
A green candle that completely covers the previous red candle. Buyers overwhelmed sellers. Strong reversal or continuation signal.
Bearish engulfing bearish
A red candle that completely covers the previous green candle. Sellers overwhelmed buyers. Strong reversal signal at resistance.
Doji neutral
Open and close are nearly equal. Buyers and sellers are in perfect balance — indecision. Often signals a reversal, especially after a long trend.
Morning star bullish
Three-candle pattern: big red → small doji → big green. Classic bottom reversal pattern. Very reliable when it appears at major support.
Candle patterns are signals, not guarantees. Always confirm with volume — a bullish engulfing on high volume is far more reliable than one on thin volume.
Technical analysis
Support and resistance
Support and resistance are the price levels where the market has repeatedly paused, reversed, or broken through. They are the most important levels on any chart.
What they are
Support (floor)
A price level where buyers have previously stepped in and pushed price back up. The more times price bounces off a level, the stronger it is. When broken, it often becomes resistance.
Resistance (ceiling)
A price level where sellers have previously pushed price back down. Price struggles to close above it. When broken with volume, it often becomes support (role reversal).
How to draw them correctly
Use candle bodies, not wicks
Draw your lines through the bodies of candles — where price spent the most time. Wicks are spikes of temporary pressure. The body is where the actual market consensus was.
Think in zones, not lines
Support and resistance are not exact prices — they're zones. If BTC bounced at 1.48 billion, 1.49 billion, and 1.50 billion on separate occasions, the support zone is 1.48–1.50 billion, not one specific number.
More touches = stronger level
A level that price has tested 4 times is stronger than one tested twice. But also note: every time a level is tested, it gets weaker. Eventually it breaks.
Role reversal — the most profitable pattern
When a resistance level is broken convincingly (with volume), it often becomes support. This is called "role reversal" and it creates one of the best entry setups — wait for price to break above resistance, then enter when it comes back to test that same level (now acting as support).
Key S/R levels to watch
Previous all-time highs/lowsMajor psychological levels, always watched
Round numbers$1B, $1.5B IDR — market clusters at round numbers
Previous swing highs/lowsRecent pivots that the market remembers
Daily/weekly open and closeInstitutional levels — watched by big players
Technical analysis
Trend lines
Trend lines are diagonal support and resistance lines that show the current direction of price. Drawing them correctly separates useful signals from misleading ones.
The three types of trends
Uptrend
Higher highs and higher lows. Draw the line connecting the lows (ascending support). Price should stay above this line.
Downtrend
Lower highs and lower lows. Draw the line connecting the highs (descending resistance). Price should stay below this line.
Sideways
Price bounces between two horizontal levels — support and resistance. Use horizontal S/R lines instead of diagonal trend lines.
How to draw trend lines correctly
Rule 1 — Minimum two points
You need at least two swing lows (for uptrend) or swing highs (for downtrend) to draw a valid trend line. Three points confirms it. One point is not a trend line — it's a guess.
Rule 2 — Connect through candle bodies
Like S/R, use candle bodies not wicks. Wicks can pierce the line slightly and still be valid — this is normal. A body close below an uptrend line is a real break.
Rule 3 — Steeper lines break faster
A trend line at 80° will break eventually. More gradual angles (30–45°) are more sustainable and more reliable. When a steep trend line breaks, price usually just moves to a shallower trend rather than reversing entirely.
Trend line breakouts — entries and exits
Bounce off trend line entry
Price pulls back to an uptrend line and bounces. Buy the bounce with a stop below the line. This is one of the cleanest setups in trading.
Break of trend line exit / reversal
A candle closes below an uptrend line on high volume. The trend is breaking. This is a signal to exit longs or look for a short setup. Wait for confirmation — one candle close below isn't always final.
Wait for a candle to close beyond the trend line before acting. Wicks often pierce trend lines as a stop hunt before reversing. A body close is confirmation.
Technical analysis
Moving averages
Moving averages smooth out price noise so you can see the trend more clearly. They also act as dynamic support and resistance levels.
MA vs EMA
Simple MA (SMA)
Takes the average of the last N candles with equal weight. Slower to react to recent price changes. Better for spotting long-term trend direction. Common: 50 SMA, 200 SMA.
Exponential MA (EMA) more popular
Gives more weight to recent candles, so it reacts faster. Better for active trading signals. Common: 9 EMA, 21 EMA, 50 EMA, 200 EMA.
Key moving averages and what they mean
9 EMA / 21 EMAShort-term momentum — used by day traders
50 EMAMedium-term trend — the most watched MA
200 EMA / 200 SMALong-term trend — bull/bear market separator
Price above 200 EMABull market bias — prefer long trades
Price below 200 EMABear market bias — prefer short trades or wait
The golden cross and death cross
Golden cross bullish
The 50 MA crosses above the 200 MA. Historically one of the most reliable long-term bullish signals in crypto. BTC has seen major rallies after every golden cross.
Death cross bearish
The 50 MA crosses below the 200 MA. A long-term bearish signal that often precedes extended downtrends. It lags behind price, so don't use it for timing exact entries.
How to use MAs for entries
MA as dynamic support
In an uptrend, price often pulls back to the 21 EMA or 50 EMA before bouncing. These pullbacks are buying opportunities — the trend is intact, price is just "resetting." Enter when price touches the EMA and shows a bounce candle (hammer, engulfing).
MA crossovers for momentum
When the 9 EMA crosses above the 21 EMA, short-term momentum is turning bullish — a signal to look for longs. When 9 EMA crosses below 21 EMA, momentum is turning bearish. Combine with volume confirmation.
MAs are lagging indicators — they show what happened, not what will happen. Always combine with price action and volume for confirmation.

RSI indicator
The Relative Strength Index measures whether a coin is overbought or oversold on a scale of 0 to 100. It tells you when a move may be getting exhausted.
RSI zones

Neutral
40–70

Oversold
0–30
Price may be too low — potential reversal zone, look for bounce setups
RSI divergence — the most powerful signal
Bullish divergence
Price makes a lower low, but RSI makes a higher low. This means selling pressure is weakening even as price drops. A strong reversal signal to the upside — look for a long entry.
Bearish divergence
Price makes a higher high, but RSI makes a lower high. Buying pressure is weakening. A strong reversal signal to the downside — consider taking profits or looking for shorts.
Practical RSI rules
RSI > 70 in a strong uptrendCan stay overbought for a long time — don't short just because RSI is high
RSI > 80Start looking for exit signals on existing longs
RSI < 30 at key supportStrong bounce setup — combine with hammer candle and volume
RSI level 50The line between bullish and bearish momentum — watch for holds/breaks here
Best timeframe for RSI4H and Daily — higher timeframe RSI signals are far more reliable
RSI alone is not a trading system. An oversold RSI during a strong downtrend can stay below 30 for weeks. Always use RSI as confirmation, not a standalone signal.
Technical analysis
MACD
MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) combines trend direction and momentum into one indicator. It tells you both which way price is moving and how strongly.
The three components
MACD line
The 12 EMA minus the 26 EMA. When positive, short-term price is above long-term — bullish momentum. When negative — bearish momentum.
Signal line
A 9 EMA of the MACD line. Slower-moving. When the MACD line crosses above it — bullish signal. When it crosses below — bearish signal.
Histogram
The difference between the MACD and signal line, shown as bars. Growing green bars = increasing bullish momentum. Growing red bars = increasing bearish momentum.
MACD signals to watch
Bullish crossover buy signal
The MACD line crosses above the signal line, especially when both are below zero (the zero line). This signals a shift from bearish to bullish momentum. Combine with a volume spike for higher confidence.
Bearish crossover sell signal
The MACD line crosses below the signal line, especially when both are above zero. Momentum is shifting bearish. A signal to consider taking profits or avoiding new longs.
Zero line cross
When the MACD line crosses from negative to positive, it means the 12 EMA has crossed above the 26 EMA — a trend shift confirmation. Crossing from positive to negative is a bearish trend shift. Slower signal but more reliable.
MACD divergence
Just like RSI, MACD divergence is powerful. Price makes higher highs but MACD makes lower highs = bearish divergence. Price makes lower lows but MACD makes higher lows = bullish divergence. Best on the 4H and Daily charts.
MACD is also a lagging indicator. By the time a crossover appears, the move may already be 20–30% done. Use it to confirm a trade you already like from price action — not to find trades by itself.
Market structure
Chart patterns
Chart patterns are formations that repeat across markets and timeframes. They form because human psychology is consistent — fear and greed create the same shapes over and over.
Reversal patterns
Head and shoulders bearish reversal
Three peaks: left shoulder, a higher head, right shoulder. Neckline connects the two lows. When price breaks below the neckline with volume, the pattern is confirmed. Target = height of the head measured from the neckline downward. One of the most reliable reversal patterns in crypto.
Inverse head and shoulders bullish reversal
The mirror image — three troughs with the middle being the deepest. Neckline connects the two highs. Breakout above neckline with volume confirms a bullish reversal. A classic bottom formation.
Double top bearish
Price hits resistance twice, fails to break above it. Forms an "M" shape. Break below the middle low confirms. Target = height of the pattern downward.
Double bottom bullish
Price tests support twice, fails to break below it. Forms a "W" shape. Break above the middle high confirms. One of the most reliable bottoming patterns.
Continuation patterns
Ascending triangle bullish
Flat resistance at top, rising support below. Price coils tighter until it breaks the resistance. Bullish — buyers are getting more aggressive each pullback.
Descending triangle bearish
Flat support at bottom, falling resistance. Sellers push lower each time. Eventually breaks support. Bearish continuation in a downtrend.
Symmetrical triangle neutral
Rising support + falling resistance. Both sides compress. Could break either way — wait for the breakout with volume to determine direction.
Bull and bear flags
Sharp move (the flagpole), followed by a brief consolidation (the flag). Break in the original direction of the flagpole is the entry. Very reliable in trending markets.
Always wait for a confirmed breakout with a candle close beyond the pattern boundary, with volume. Many patterns fail right before confirmation. Patience here saves a lot of money.
Market structure
Market structure
Market structure is the framework of how price moves — a sequence of highs and lows that tells you who is in control: buyers or sellers.
The two market structures
Bullish structure
Higher highs (HH) + higher lows (HL). Each swing high is above the previous, each pullback stops higher than the last. Buyers are in control. Only take long trades when structure is bullish.
Bearish structure
Lower highs (LH) + lower lows (LL). Each swing high is below the previous, each bounce gets rejected sooner. Sellers are in control. Only take short trades when structure is bearish.
Break of structure (BOS) — the most important signal
What a break of structure means
In a bullish structure, when price breaks below the most recent higher low — that's a break of structure. It means the uptrend may be ending. In a bearish structure, when price breaks above the most recent lower high — the downtrend may be reversing. BOS is the earliest signal of a trend change.
Change of character (CHoCH)
The confirmation of a trend reversal
After a break of structure, if price then creates a new swing high (in a previous downtrend), that's a change of character — buyers are now making higher highs. This is when most smart traders begin entering long positions. CHoCH = structure confirmed, not just broken.
Market phases
AccumulationSmart money buying while price is low and sideways
Markup (uptrend)Price rises — HH and HL structure. Retail notices and buys late
DistributionSmart money selling while price is high and sideways
Markdown (downtrend)Price falls — LH and LL structure. Retail sells late
Most retail traders buy at the top of markup (when everyone is excited) and sell at the bottom of markdown (when everyone is panicking). Market structure helps you see where you actually are in the cycle.

On-chain analysis
On-chain analysis reads actual blockchain data — wallet movements, exchange flows, and whale activity. It's information the chart alone can't give you.
Why on-chain matters
Every crypto transaction is recorded on a public blockchain. Unlike traditional finance, you can see exactly how many coins are moving, where they're going, and what wallets are doing. This data often leads price moves rather than following them.
Key on-chain metrics
Exchange inflows and outflows
Coins moving TO exchanges: usually a sell signal — people are depositing to sell.
Coins moving FROM exchanges: usually a buy signal — people are withdrawing to hold long-term. This reduces sell pressure.

When large amounts of BTC leave exchanges, it often precedes price increases.
Whale wallet tracking
Wallets holding large amounts of BTC or ETH (1,000+ BTC) are tracked publicly. When whales accumulate quietly (small buys over time), price often rises later. When whales send large amounts to exchanges, a sell-off may follow. Tools like Whale Alert and Lookonchain track this in real time.
SOPR — Spent Output Profit Ratio
Measures whether coins being moved are in profit or loss. SOPR above 1 = people are selling at a profit. SOPR below 1 = people are selling at a loss (capitulation). SOPR dropping below 1 and then recovering is often a bottom signal.
Active addresses
The number of unique wallets active on a given day. Rising active addresses with rising price = genuine network growth. Rising price with falling active addresses = speculative price increase without real user adoption — often unsustainable.

On-chain analysis is best as a macro filter — it tells you whether the environment favors buying or selling. Combine it with technical analysis for entries and exits.
Psychology and strategy
FOMO and FUD
FOMO and FUD are the two emotional forces that cause most retail traders to lose money. Understanding them intellectually isn't enough — you need systems to counter them.
FOMO — Fear of Missing Out
What FOMO looks like
BTC pumps 15% in 2 hours. Twitter is euphoric. You feel like if you don't buy right now, you'll miss the biggest move of the year. You buy at the top. Price reverses immediately. You panic sell at a loss. This is FOMO — and it's a designed feature of crypto markets, not a bug.
Why FOMO hurts so much in crypto
Crypto moves faster than any other market. A 30% move can happen in hours. This triggers FOMO more intensely than stocks. And social media — Twitter/X, Telegram, Reddit — amplifies it. Every influencer posting about gains feeds the fear that you're behind. Most of them bought much earlier and are now distributing into your FOMO buy.
The FOMO antidote
There will always be another trade. Missing this one move doesn't mean you missed everything. The traders who survive long-term are the ones who wait for their setups — not the ones who chase every pump. Write this down: "If I didn't plan this trade before the move started, I don't take it."
FUD — Fear, Uncertainty, and Doubt
What FUD looks like
Negative news hits — a country bans crypto, an exchange is hacked, a major coin collapses. Price dumps 20%. Headlines scream that crypto is dead (again). You sell everything at the bottom. Price recovers two weeks later. This is FUD — and markets exploit it systematically.
The FUD antidote
Ask: does this news change the long-term fundamentals of BTC or ETH? Usually the answer is no. Bad news is often the best buying opportunity for those who can stay calm. Check your timeframe — if you're a swing trader holding for 3–5 days, short-term FUD news is noise. If you're a long-term holder, a 20% dip on news is usually irrelevant in a year.
Systems to protect yourself
Pre-trade checklistWrite the plan before entry. If emotion changes the plan, don't trade.
No social media during tradingTwitter and Telegram are FOMO/FUD machines. Check after, not during.
24-hour rule for news eventsWait 24 hours before acting on major news. Let the knee-jerk reaction pass.
Position size as emotional controlSmaller positions = less emotion. You can't FOMO or panic on a 1% position.
The trading journalAfter 3 months, your FOMO trades and FUD exits will show up clearly in the data.
The market is designed to take your money. It pumps to trigger FOMO at the top, and dumps to trigger fear at the bottom. Being aware of this doesn't make you immune — but it gives you a fighting chance.
Psychology and strategy
DCA vs active trading
Honest comparison of two very different approaches. One is boring and works for most people. The other is exciting and works for very few.
Dollar Cost Averaging (DCA)
What DCA is
Buy a fixed amount of BTC or ETH at regular intervals — every week or every month — regardless of price. No timing, no analysis, no stress. You automatically buy more when price is low and less when price is high, averaging your cost down over time.
DCA vs Active trading — side by side
Time requiredDCA: 5 min/month  |  Trading: 1–4 hours/day
Skill requiredDCA: None  |  Trading: 6–12 months to learn
Emotional stressDCA: Low  |  Trading: High
% of people who profitDCA: Most (long term)  |  Trading: ~20–30%
Potential upsideDCA: Market returns  |  Trading: Market returns × skill
Risk of total lossDCA: Very low  |  Trading: Moderate to high
Works during bear marketDCA: Yes (accumulating)  |  Trading: Only with shorting
For your situation (Rp 4jt/month income, savings goal)
Recommendation: DCA into BTC and ETH as your base strategy. Allocate Rp 400,000–600,000/month automatically. This builds your portfolio consistently without the time or emotional cost of trading. If you also want to learn trading, practice with a small portion (Rp 200,000) while learning on paper first.
The most honest comparison
A 4-year DCA into BTC
If you had put Rp 400,000 into BTC every month starting in January 2020, by end of 2023 you would have invested roughly Rp 19 million and your position would be worth multiples of that — despite the 2022 bear market. You would have done this without watching a single chart. Most active traders underperformed this benchmark during the same period.
What active trading actually requires
To beat DCA through active trading, you need to be right more than the market return. That requires discipline, skill, emotional control, a solid strategy, risk management, and a trading journal — consistently, for years. Most people who try quit within 6 months. The ones who succeed treat it like learning a professional skill, not a get-rich scheme.
There's no wrong answer. Many successful crypto participants do both: DCA a core position in BTC/ETH, and actively trade a smaller "learning" allocation. The DCA protects them while the trading allocation educates them.
